\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:exp}

\subsection{Experimental setup}
\label{sec:setup}

\textbf{Data and protocol.} All experiments use the TrajGap-Bench Mixed protocol of Section~\ref{sec:bench}: Mixed-train for fitting, Mixed-val for checkpoint selection on $\mathrm{minFDE}_{20}$ of the final head, Mixed-test evaluated once, five scenes, $T=8$, $H=12$, $K=20$. One model is trained per scene, following the release's per-scene splits. Every method is trained on the natural Mixed distribution; no method re-weights samples by missing count.

\textbf{Implementation.} GAST uses an embedding width of 128, 8 attention heads, four selective state-space blocks in the temporal stack, two cross-attention layers in the decoder, $K=20$ modes and dropout/drop-path 0.2. Training uses AdamW with learning rate $10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, 5 warm-up epochs, gradient clipping at norm 5, batch size 64 and bf16 mixed precision for at most 100 epochs; the selected checkpoints occur between epochs 33 and 58, so the budget is not binding. Each scene is trained on a single NVIDIA RTX 5880 Ada GPU. The model has 1.19M parameters. The seed is fixed to 2024 for all methods.

\textbf{Baselines.} We compare against eight methods, spanning three families.
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Internal baselines.} \textbf{M0} is our previous forecaster: a recurrent history encoder, a stack of scene-interaction blocks and the standard mode-query decoder, trained under an identical protocol. \textbf{UT-ENC} replaces the M0 encoder with the past-encoder backbone that GAST builds on, while keeping the standard decoder; it isolates the effect of adopting the backbone from the effect of the evidence machinery.
\item \emph{Complete-history forecasters.} \textbf{SocialLSTM} \cite{alahi2016social} is run in a missing-aware variant (zero placeholder, LSTM state update skipped at missing frames, Gaussian-mixture head with $K=20$). \textbf{AgentFormer} \cite{yuan2021agentformer} receives an explicit per-frame validity channel and anchors the decoder at the last observed frame. \textbf{MoFlow} \cite{fu2025moflow} uses its official implementation with mask-aware features and a per-channel mean placeholder at missing steps. \textbf{Re} \cite{mao2025resonance} is run unmodified with zero-filled histories.
\item \emph{Imputation-aware forecaster.} \textbf{MS-TIP} \cite{chib2024mstip} couples a SAITS-based imputation branch with the predictor and is trained end-to-end; it represents the impute-and-predict family under the direct input constraint. \textbf{Social-STGCNN} \cite{mohamed2020social} is included as a graph-based reference; missing agents are represented as ghost nodes that neither send nor receive messages.
\end{itemize}
Methods without a mode-probability head (AgentFormer, Re, MS-TIP, Social-STGCNN) are excluded from the top-1 diagnostics but included in the primary metrics, which only require $K$ candidates. All methods see exactly the same test tensors.

\subsection{Main results}
\label{sec:main}

Table~\ref{tab:main} reports the primary comparison. GAST obtains the best macro average on both metrics, 0.3168/0.4831~m, and the best micro average, 0.2894/0.4716~m. Relative to the strongest external baseline (MoFlow), this is a reduction of 9.4\% in $\mathrm{minADE}_{20}$ and 7.6\% in $\mathrm{minFDE}_{20}$ on the macro average, and 7.5\%/5.3\% on the micro average. Relative to the strongest internal baseline (M0), the reduction is 1.7\%/1.8\% macro and 5.9\%/6.2\% micro. GAST is better than MoFlow on all five scenes for $\mathrm{minADE}_{20}$ and on four of five for $\mathrm{minFDE}_{20}$, the exception being ZARA1-M, where MoFlow is 1.8\% better.

The per-scene picture is more informative than the aggregate. GAST attains the best $\mathrm{minFDE}_{20}$ on ETH-M, UNIV-M and ZARA2-M, and the best $\mathrm{minADE}_{20}$ on ETH-M and UNIV-M. On HOTEL-M, AgentFormer is clearly better (0.2083/0.3316 against 0.2541/0.3769), and on ZARA1-M, M0 is better on both metrics. GAST is therefore better than M0 on three scenes for $\mathrm{minFDE}_{20}$ and on two for $\mathrm{minADE}_{20}$, while its macro average improves because its gains on ETH-M and UNIV-M are larger than its losses on the other scenes. We do not claim a uniform improvement, and Section~\ref{sec:disc} discusses what distinguishes the scenes on which the method helps.

The two weakest baselines are far behind. MS-TIP, the representative of imputation-aware joint training, is the second worst method, and Social-STGCNN, which represents unobserved agents as ghost nodes, is the worst. Both are trained under the same contract as every other method, so the gap is not a protocol artefact; it reflects the difficulty of the 8-frame setting, in which up to seven frames are absent and the gaps frequently reach the forecast origin. The classical social-pooling model is also well behind (0.4576/0.6079), which is expected given the short history and the absence of explicit multi-modality in its missing-aware variant.

\subsection{Behaviour under increasing missingness}
\label{sec:severity}

Table~\ref{tab:severity} and Figure~\ref{fig:degrad} stratify the test set by the number of missing history frames. The benchmark's official sweep assigns equal test mass to each count from 0 to 7 (33{,}654 samples per bucket, with 67{,}308 for four because ETH-M contributes two four-frame buckets), which makes the buckets directly comparable.

The curves separate the methods in a way that the aggregate does not. GAST degrades most gracefully from zero to six missing frames: its $\mathrm{minFDE}_{20}$ rises from 0.3592 to 0.4281~m (+19\%), against +30\% for M0, +34\% for UT-ENC and +46\% for MoFlow. The relative advantage over M0 is not constant but grows with severity: $+1.0\%$ at zero missing, $-0.5\%$ at two, $-3.9\%$ at five and $-7.3\%$ at six. This monotone dose--response pattern is the strongest evidence we have that the improvement is produced by the evidence machinery rather than by extra capacity, since a capacity effect would not concentrate in the buckets where evidence is scarce.

At seven missing frames, only a single observation survives. All methods collapse to 1.22--1.31~m and the ordering changes: MoFlow is best (1.2224), GAST second at 1.2731, M0 at 1.3073. With one frame there is no velocity, no heading and no gap structure to exploit, so the forecast is determined by the learned prior, and a method regularised for evidence routing has little left to route. We regard this as a boundary of the regime rather than a failure of the model, but it does mean that GAST should not be advertised as improving the single-frame case.

The fully observed bucket deserves separate comment, because it is the only condition in which GAST is slightly worse than its own predecessor on $\mathrm{minFDE}_{20}$ ($+1.0\%$) while being better on $\mathrm{minADE}_{20}$ ($-1.8\%$). This is consistent with the exact-reduction property of Section~\ref{sec:method}: when no frame is missing, the evidence state is an identity, and the remaining difference is the goal-conditioned decoder, which trades a small endpoint bias for better average coverage. Section~\ref{sec:disc} returns to this point.

\subsection{Terminal missingness and anchor lag}
\label{sec:lag}

Table~\ref{tab:lag} reports the same comparison along the two axes that the missing count confounds. Terminal missingness separates samples whose gap reaches the forecast origin from those whose gap ends earlier, and anchor lag counts the steps between the last observation and the origin.

The terminal group is where the difference between the two models is clearest: GAST reduces $\mathrm{minFDE}_{20}$ by 3.6\% relative to M0 on samples with a terminal gap, against a $+0.8\%$ difference on the non-terminal group. This is the condition that the anchor state and the progress-conditioned skeleton were designed for, and it is also the condition in which a model anchored at a placeholder row is most exposed.

Along anchor lag, GAST is better than M0 at every lag from 1 to 6, with the largest gaps at lags 4 and 5 ($-8.8\%$ and $-5.5\%$), but it is worse at lag 7 ($+3.2\%$), the regime in which only the first frame of the window is observed and the anchor is eight steps old. This crossing is the same phenomenon as the seven-missing bucket: beyond a certain evidence age, the estimate of the anchor velocity is dominated by noise, and a prior-driven model can be more accurate than an evidence-driven one. It suggests that the reliability decay should eventually saturate rather than continue to favour a very old state, which we leave to future work.

\subsection{Build-up ablation}
\label{sec:ablation}

Table~\ref{tab:ablation} traces the model from M0 through UT-ENC to GAST. Replacing the M0 encoder with the past-encoder backbone alone (UT-ENC) does not improve the benchmark: its macro error is 1.3\%/0.6\% worse than M0. The gain appears only when the evidence state is routed through the encoder and the anchor-conditioned decoder is added: GAST improves over UT-ENC by 2.9\%/2.3\% macro and 1.7\%/1.5\% micro, and over M0 by 1.7\%/1.8\% macro.

This ordering matters for the interpretation of the method. It shows that the improvement is not attributable to the backbone that GAST borrows from complete-history forecasting \cite{xu2025sportstraj}; if it were, UT-ENC would already capture it. What changes the outcome is the gap-specific treatment of that backbone, together with the decoder. We must, however, be explicit about the limitation of this ablation: it is a build-up sequence in which the encoder and the decoder change together between UT-ENC and GAST, so it does not isolate GERE from ECTA from the goal decoder. A strictly controlled four-arm ablation (backbone, backbone+GERE/ECTA, backbone+decoder, full) is the natural next experiment, and we report the current sequence as evidence of the direction rather than as a component-level attribution. UT-ENC was also trained under the earlier checkpoint monitor, before the final-head rule of Section~\ref{sec:contract} was fixed; the discrepancy concerns checkpoint selection only and is documented together with the released artefacts.

\subsection{Coverage versus ranking}
\label{sec:coverage}

Table~\ref{tab:top1} reports the top-1 diagnostics. GAST's $\mathrm{ADE@1}$/$\mathrm{FDE@1}$ are 0.9806/1.7887~m, which is 10.9\%/12.8\% worse than M0 and 10.2\%/12.7\% worse than UT-ENC, while its best-of-20 metrics are the best in the comparison. The dissociation is systematic: it holds for every scene and it is not shared by the encoder-only variants.

Two observations constrain the explanation. First, the regression is confined to the top-1 decision; the candidate set itself is better, as the primary metrics show. Second, the models with a standard decoder (M0, UT-ENC) have both better top-1 and worse best-of-20, which points to the decoder rather than the encoder as the source of the trade-off. Our working hypothesis is that the goal-conditioned decoder, trained with an explicit diversity margin on endpoints and with trajectory-level mode labels, spreads the mode set and assigns probability mass more evenly, which improves coverage and simultaneously reduces the mass on the single best mode. This is a hypothesis about a mechanism, not a demonstrated causal explanation; we report it as an open problem, since a forecaster that is deployed behind a planner is usually consumed through its highest-probability mode, and a 12\% degradation there is material.

\subsection{Reproducibility}
\label{sec:repro}

The protocol, the Mixed adapter, the training runner, the evaluator and the model configuration are released as a single repository. Every run writes a manifest containing the git revision, the resolved configuration, the data files consumed, the checkpoint rule and the environment (Python, PyTorch and CUDA versions). Evaluation emits a JSON file with per-sample diagnostics for each of the five stratification axes, from which all tables in this paper are generated by script rather than transcribed by hand. The Laplace likelihood has two numerically equivalent implementations, one of which is vectorised; an equivalence gate verifies that their gradients agree to machine precision, and the implementation used by each run is recorded in its manifest.
